## Gate Keeping the Stone 

Welcome to Gatekeeping the Stone, a research repository examining how institutional power structures control, filter, and transmute knowledge in the modern academy and beyond. The title draws on the alchemical symbol of the Philosopher's Stone—that substance capable of transmuting base material into gold and granting access to hidden wisdom. Today, epistemic gatekeepers—universities, publishers, indexing platforms, and disciplinary cartels—position themselves as guardians of this stone, determining whose knowledge is refined, whose is discarded, and who may participate in the great work of collective understanding.

Hosted within the Lightning Path ecosystem, this repository collects critical resources analyzing the architecture of epistemic control. It documents how institutional gatekeeping functions not merely as administrative procedure but as a technology of power that domesticates or erases diverse epistemologies, marginalizes independent scholarship, and transforms knowledge from a public good into a privatized credential. Here you will find curated theoretical foundations, applied critiques, and alternative frameworks for understanding how knowledge is kept—and how it might be freed.

To submit a theoretical paper or case study to this repository, contact mikes@athabascau.ca

## Submissions

We welcome contributions that document, analyze, or theorize the epistemic violence of modern scholarly communication. Suitable submissions include:

* Theoretical papers and critical essays
* Case studies of institutional gatekeeping, exclusion, or epistemic injustice
* Survey research and empirical datasets
* Articles examining the structures, policies, and practices that constrain knowledge production and dissemination

Requirements: All submitted materials must be publicly accessible and openly available. By submitting a resource to this repository, you agree to place it in the public domain. Links to scholarly journal articles are permitted provided they are clearly flagged as open-access or otherwise freely available without paywall or subscription.

To submit a paper, dataset, or resource, please contact mikes@athabascau.ca.
